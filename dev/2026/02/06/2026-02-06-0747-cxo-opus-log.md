### 8:38 AM - History Sidebar Flattening Response

Received Lead Developer memo identifying History Sidebar flattening pattern — both sidebars showing same data despite Layer 2 vision.

**CXO Design Responses to Four Questions:**

1. **Screen real estate**: Two sidebars serving different *cognitive functions* is defensible. Left = present/future ("what am I working on?"), Right = past/accumulated ("what have I learned?")

2. **Entity surfacing**: Yes, right sidebar should evolve to surface WorkItems, Documents, People with lifecycle states. Conversations become context for entities, not primary content.

3. **Archive vs. Active**: Framing language matters — "Conversations" vs "History/Memory", recent-first vs search-first, resume vs find/recall

4. **Trust gradient**: Visible but explained, not hidden. Locked features show with explanation; unlocks are celebrated. Supports "assistant proving themselves" framing.

**Agreed with Cathedral Document recommendation** but flagged methodology questions before drafting.

### 8:52 AM - PPM Visibility Recommendation

PM noted PPM leaning toward hiding right sidebar during MVP (bias to action). PM prefers CXO approach of hinting at possibilities.

Created `memo-from-cxo-to-ppm-history-sidebar-visibility-2026-02-06.md` arguing for middle path:

**Core argument**: Distinguish between "broken" (hide), "incomplete" (show with framing), and "duplicative" (differentiate or hide). The sidebar is duplicative but fixable with modest effort.

**Minimum viable differentiation**:
- Wire up existing search UI
- Change framing language ("History" not "Conversations")
- Archive-oriented feel vs. active-context feel

**Identity argument**: "Piper is learning to do X" > "Piper can't do X" — aligns with assistant-proving-themselves framing.

**Proposed decision heuristic**: Get Lead Dev estimate. If <2 hours, visible-but-differentiated. If >1 day, hiding is probably right.

Memo framed as collaborative perspective, not veto of product judgment.
