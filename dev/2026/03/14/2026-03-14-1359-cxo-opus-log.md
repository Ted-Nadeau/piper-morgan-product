## Work Completed

### 1. Roundtable Memo: The Floor Problem
Wrote CXO perspective memo for roundtable discussion. Core argument:

**The intent classifier acts as a bouncer, not a concierge.** We designed for the ceiling (excellent curated workflows) and accidentally demolished the floor (basic conversational competence). The worst-case Piper experience is worse than the worst-case chat-wrapper experience.

**Proposed principle**: The floor must always be "at least as good as an LLM with context." No query should ever produce "I can't do that." The classifier becomes a router, not a gate — match goes to handler, no match goes to LLM with full Piper context.

**One-thing-tomorrow recommendation**: Route unmatched queries to the LLM with Piper's project context instead of to the generic fallback. The classifier stays, handlers stay, everything we've built is the ceiling. But the floor becomes "Piper will always talk with you."

Posed specific questions for each roundtable participant (Architect on feasibility, PPM on roadmap impact, CIO on pattern naming and methodology connection).

Noted irony: yesterday's contextual fallback copy (8 bespoke rejection messages) is a band-aid on exactly this wound. The real fix isn't better rejection copy — it's not rejecting at all.

**Deliverable**: `memo-cxo-floor-problem-roundtable-2026-03-14.md`

### 2. Roundtable Memo Review (Architect, PPM, CIO)
Reviewed three companion memos:

- **Architect**: Confirmed the routing change is architecturally bounded (one new terminal node). Raised four honest risks: confidence boundary, expectation management, context quality, slippery slope. The feasibility confirmation is the key signal.
- **PPM**: Layer inversion framing — "We built Layer 1 (handlers) without Layer 0 (conversation)." Clearest structural articulation of the problem.
- **CIO**: Connected to "Day 100 agent," AX testing, and Pattern-062. Named the learning intake loop as next step after the floor.

**Key observation**: 4/4 unanimous on both diagnosis and one-thing-tomorrow. Strongest consensus in any roundtable to date.

### 3. PPM Synthesis Review
Reviewed PPM's roundtable synthesis memo. Provided two pieces of feedback:

1. **Expectation management needs an owner.** The Architect flagged it as a risk, the synthesis listed it under "risks worth discussing" but not under "recommended actions." Recommended adding CXO voice guidance for conversational-floor responses to the "soon" actions. Accepted ownership.

2. **Instrumentation emphasis.** Endorsed PPM's recommendation to measure how often users hit UNKNOWN. This data drives handler prioritization and is effectively the Layer 2 learning loop operating before it's formally built.
