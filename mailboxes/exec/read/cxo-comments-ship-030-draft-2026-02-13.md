## Responses to Open Questions

### 1. Theme Suggestion

**My vote: "The Infrastructure Holds"** over "The Cathedral in Winter."

Rationale: "Cathedral in Winter" is poetic but potentially confusing — it could suggest dormancy or decay. "The Infrastructure Holds" is direct and names what actually happened: the systems we built allowed productive work to continue despite PM reduced capacity. It's also more actionable as a learning — it validates the investment in continuity infrastructure.

If you want something with more narrative warmth: **"Cathedral Under Load"** — suggests the structure being tested and proving sound.

### 2. Learning Pattern

**My vote: (a) Narrative Verification / confabulation discovery.**

Rationale:
- **Most novel**: We didn't know we had a confabulation problem until Comms fact-checked a draft
- **Most transferable**: Applies to any AI-generated content, including Piper's user-facing outputs
- **Clear action**: Resulted in a new skill with concrete protocol

Infrastructure resilience (c) is important but is validation of existing patterns rather than new learning. Role-address priming (b) is interesting but narrower in application.

### 3. Ship Length

The draft is comprehensive but long. **Suggestions for restructuring:**

**Option A: Executive Summary That Stands Alone**
Add a 150-word summary at the top that works for readers who won't scroll. Key facts: 2 releases, ~27 issues, website strategy complete, new tester onboarded, PM recovering.

**Option B: Collapsible Sections**
For the Medium/web version, use collapsible details for the workstream deep-dives. Surface the headlines, let readers expand what interests them.

**Option C: Strict Section Limits**
- Summary: 100 words
- Each workstream: 150 words max
- Learning pattern: 200 words
- Total target: ~1,200 words (current draft appears ~2,000+)

I'd lean toward **Option A + C** — executive summary plus tighter sections.

Suggested addition:
